The problem

Enterprises have the data. Meaning is scattered.

The knowledge needed to use enterprise data is spread across systems, teams and years of implementation history.

Customer, order, product, inventory, support and operational data may live in different systems, use different identifiers, and have relationships that are obvious only to people who have worked with those systems for years.

The same estate may span transactional data, documents, events and high-volume time-series data across very different storage and processing technologies.

The idea

Can we recover that meaning from the estate itself?

DataSong is exploring whether the information an organisation already has can be used to reconstruct a first semantic model of a business domain — entities, relationships and the physical data behind them.

The aim is not to replace domain experts. It is to give them a strong starting point that can be inspected, corrected and trusted.

Existing data

The structures and patterns already present across enterprise datasets.

System knowledge

The metadata and context already embedded in the systems that use that data.

Human review

A semantic model that domain experts can verify rather than build from a blank page.

Why we are doing this

This comes from years spent inside large enterprise data estates.

Our experience spans large enterprise datasets, data platforms, pipelines, workflows, analytics and machine-learning systems. We have repeatedly seen how much effort goes into finding the right data, understanding its context and reconnecting knowledge that already exists somewhere in the organisation.

DataSong started from a simple question: with the capabilities now available, can much more of that discovery happen automatically?

The pilot

We want to test this against a real, bounded enterprise problem.

We are looking for one or two organisations willing to explore a small business domain with us. A pilot might connect customer, orders and inventory data with operational or machine time-series data — potentially spread across SQL or NoSQL systems, a warehouse, and Delta Lake or object storage — and test how much of the underlying semantic model can be reconstructed from the systems already in place.

Small and practical

  • One bounded business or operational domain
  • A manageable set of datasets and systems
  • Structured and time-series data where relevant
  • Read-only discovery and expert review

Designed to be low-risk

  • Run within the organisation's environment where required
  • Work under NDA
  • No production workflow changes
  • The goal is simply to measure what can be discovered
What success looks like

A useful semantic picture, not another technology project.

If the experiment works, the result should be a browsable model of the business concepts in that domain, how they relate, and where the supporting data lives — with enough context for an expert to understand why those relationships were proposed.

From there, the longer-term question is whether analysts, data engineers and ML teams can work from that shared business meaning instead of repeatedly rebuilding the same understanding in pipelines and projects.
