the learning enterprise map

A living map of how the enterprise works.

lemap learns the relationships between enterprise data and the workflows that give that data business meaning — then makes the resulting map explorable and queryable for ML, analytics, operations, engineering and decision-making.

working experiment

We gave lemap an unfamiliar open-source ecommerce application and let it figure out how the business works.

No hand-built semantic model. lemap inspected the application's executable structure and data relationships, discovered business workflows, and built a persistent semantic map connecting the two.

why the query matters

The useful part is not finding a table. It is finding the business context around the data.

A conventional catalog can tell you that fields and tables exist. lemap also learns how those entities participate in workflows — how an order is created, how a customer is connected to it, what gets persisted, and which surrounding relationships matter.

So a query can start with business intent and navigate toward the relevant entities, relationships and learned workflows instead of forcing the user to reconstruct the application first.

what

The enterprise already contains its own map. It is just scattered across systems.

Relationships live in schemas and persistent objects. Business behaviour lives in applications and workflows. Operational meaning accumulates in documents, messages and people's heads.

data+workflows+applications+documents+conversations→enterprise semantic map

lemap is intended to learn that structure continuously rather than requiring people to remodel the enterprise manually every time they need a new view or answer.

One shared map → data views for ML and analytics · business questions · operational tracing · support context · engineering understanding

how

It learns much like a new engineer joining the company.

Inspect what is already there. Follow relationships and executable paths. Form a view of what the business is doing. Check it against more evidence. Keep updating the map.

Today the working experiment learns from application structure, data relationships and executable workflows. The larger map is designed to absorb more enterprise evidence over time.

use

Ask for the business view instead of first reconstructing the technology underneath it.

Business decisionsAsk questions in business terms and navigate toward the data and processes that support the answer.

EngineeringUnderstand how business behaviour spans services, persistent objects and application flows before changing it.
