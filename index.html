Why

Enterprise knowledge is scattered.

The business already knows what its data means and how its processes work. That knowledge is simply spread across systems and people.

Relationships live in databases. Business processes live in applications and workflows. Exceptions and operational knowledge live in emails, documents and conversations. Much of the context needed to use enterprise data is tribal knowledge.

Every analyst, engineer, ML team or new employee ends up reconstructing parts of the same picture.

What

A connected semantic map that keeps learning.

DataSong continuously gathers evidence from enterprise sources and connects business concepts, data, workflows, systems and knowledge into one evolving map.

Business conceptsEntitiesRelationshipsDataWorkflowsSystemsPersistent objectsOperational knowledge

The map becomes a shared layer from which different users can ask for the information they need without first understanding every underlying system.

ML engineersData analystsDecision makersOperationsSupportEngineers
How

It learns like a new engineer joining the company.

A good engineer does not start by asking someone to model the whole enterprise. They inspect what is already there, follow how things work, form hypotheses, ask questions and connect evidence until a useful mental model emerges.

DataSong attempts to automate the same process.

Inspect data, code and other evidence
Follow workflows and relationships
Form business hypotheses
Check them against more evidence
Update the enterprise map

When code changes, new data appears or new knowledge is captured, the map can learn from that evidence rather than being rebuilt manually.

Live experiment

Give it an application it has never seen.

As a first experiment, DataSong is given an open-source ecommerce application repository and asked to work out what the business does.

DataSong reads the repository and builds the map as it learns.

It traces executable paths, reconstructs business flows, identifies entities and persistent objects, and connects the evidence into business arcs.

Examples discovered: checkout · product search and cart · orders · payments · customer profile · inventory · returns · shipping · accounting

Live demo coming here. The map can be viewed while discovery is still running — it does not need to wait for the application to be completely understood.

The result

Ask for the business view, not the pipeline.

Once the map understands where concepts live and how they connect, users can work from business meaning instead of reconstructing the technology underneath it.

An analyst can ask for a combined view. An ML engineer can ask for the data needed for a feature or model. A decision maker can ask a business question. Operations and support teams can trace how a process, customer or transaction moves through the enterprise.

The long-term goal is simple: make the accumulated intelligence of the enterprise directly usable.

We are looking for a small number of enterprise pilots.
