the learning enterprise map

A living map of how the enterprise works.

Enterprise knowledge is scattered across data, applications, code, workflows, documents, emails and conversations. lemap continuously learns what these pieces mean and how they relate, building a shared enterprise mind for ML, analytics, decision-making, operations, support and engineering teams.

why

Using enterprise information usually begins with rediscovering what it means and how the business fits together.

Data relationships live in databases. Processes live in applications and workflows. Exceptions and operational knowledge live in documents, emails, conversations and people's heads.

Enormous time goes into repeatedly reconstructing this meaning — building data pipelines and views for analytics and ML, combining data across systems for questions such as sales, shipments or customers, tracing the impact of process and application changes, understanding how business workflows actually operate, and rediscovering tribal knowledge buried in documents, emails, conversations and people's heads.

Much of this work starts with the same problem: understanding what things mean and how they connect.

what

lemap learns that meaning, keeps it current, and makes it available as a shared enterprise mind.

data+workflows+code+documents+conversations→enterprise semantic map

The map connects business concepts, entities, relationships, data, workflows, systems, persistent objects and operational knowledge.

One shared map → data views for ML and analytics · business questions · operational tracing · support context · engineering understanding

how

It learns much like a new engineer joining the company.

Inspect what is already there. Follow workflows and relationships. Form hypotheses about what the business is doing. Check them against more evidence. Keep updating the map.

01inspect evidence
02follow relationships
03form hypotheses
04check evidence
05update the map

New code, data or captured knowledge becomes new evidence. The map evolves instead of being manually remodelled.

see it learn

We gave lemap an unfamiliar open-source ecommerce application repository.

It reads the executable structure, works out candidate business flows, follows the evidence and progressively reconstructs the business map.

As it runs, lemap discovers business flows and connects their steps, entities, persistent objects, relationships and outcomes.

use

Ask for the business view instead of first reconstructing the technology underneath it.

An analyst can ask for a combined data view. An ML engineer can ask for the data needed for a model. A decision maker can ask a business question. Operations and support can trace a process, customer or transaction across systems.
